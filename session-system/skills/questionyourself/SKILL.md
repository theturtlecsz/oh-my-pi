---
name: questionyourself
description: Honest self-audit of just-completed work. Use when the user wants a candid confidence check after a task, or asks "what are you least confident about?".
---

Stop and answer this question honestly, in plain prose:

**What are you least confident about in what you just did?**

Rules for the answer:

- Interrogate the work you just completed, not the plan. Name the specific claim, change, or conclusion that rests on the weakest evidence.
- Distinguish what you verified (ran, read, measured) from what you assumed, inferred, or took on trust. Unverified steps get named explicitly.
- If several things are shaky, rank them — least confident first — and say why each is shaky.
- For each item, state what would increase confidence (a command to run, a file to re-check, a test to add) — and if it's cheap, just do it instead of only proposing it.
- If the honest answer is "nothing material — everything load-bearing was verified," say that and show why, briefly. Do not manufacture doubt.
- No hedging language, no reassurance. Candor over comfort.
